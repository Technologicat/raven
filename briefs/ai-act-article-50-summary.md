# EU AI Act — Article 50 transparency rules

Commission guidelines adopted 20 July 2026.

Applies from **2 August 2026**.

**Scope note:** obligations attach to supplying a system for distribution or use in the EU, **whether free of charge or for payment**. No fee involved doesn't mean out of scope.

**Open source:** the free/open-source exemption (Article 2(12)) does **not** cover Article 50 — FOSS-licensed systems are still in scope if they fall under Article 5 (prohibited practices) or 50 (transparency).

## Provider obligations

(whoever develops a system and puts it on the market under their own name)

- **50(1):** systems that interact directly with people must tell people they're dealing with an AI — at first interaction, clearly, and accessibly. Exception if it's obvious, interpreted narrowly.
- **50(2):** outputs of generative systems (text, image, audio, video) must carry machine-readable marks so the content is detectable as AI-generated. Out of scope: source code, very short symbol/letter sequences, machine-to-machine-only outputs, closed-loop industrial use, and standard editing assistance.

## Deployer obligations

(whoever runs the system in a professional capacity — excludes purely personal use)

- **50(3):** inform people exposed to emotion recognition or biometric categorisation.
- **50(4):** label deepfakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Doesn't apply if the text had genuine human review or editorial control — spell-checking doesn't count.

## Deadlines

- **2 Aug 2026:** everything applies.
- **2 Dec 2026:** grace period for 50(2) marking only, and only for systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026.
- Content generated before 2 Aug 2026 needs no retroactive labelling.

## Enforcement

By national market surveillance authorities. Fines up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover, with proportionality for SMEs.

There's a voluntary Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content, approved by the Commission and AI Board — signing it is a recognised way to demonstrate compliance with the marking obligations.
