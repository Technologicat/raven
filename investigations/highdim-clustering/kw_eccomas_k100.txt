### 1. Predictive Modeling of Intraoperative Hypotension (IOH)
*   **Objective:** To develop a robust methodology for predicting IOH during general anesthesia using physiological signals, addressing limitations in existing machine learning (ML) approaches.
*   **Methodology:**
    *   Investigated physiological signal-based prediction.
    *   Addressed critiques of previous ML studies regarding database bias and lack of relevant baselines.
    *   Utilized the open-source **VitalDB database**.
    *   Applied standard ML algorithms and integrated **explainability** techniques to assist anesthesiologists.
*   **Key Insight:** The work emphasizes the need for rigorous evaluation against baselines and highlights the clinical utility of explainable AI in high-stakes surgical environments.

### 2. Patient-Specific In Silico Model for Radiation-Induced Pulmonary Fibrosis
*   **Objective:** To predict the development of pulmonary fibrosis (PF) following radiation therapy (RT) for non-small cell lung cancer, improving upon limited demographic-based prognostic models.
*   **Methodology:**
    *   Developed a **patient-specific 3D model** governed by a system of partial differential equations (PDEs).
    *   Simulated interactions between cancerous cells, fibroblasts, and lung parenchyma.
    *   Inputs included CT-derived geometries, Hounsfield data, radiation dosage distribution, and lesion position for **10 patients**.
*   **Results:**
    *   Simulations reproduced clinically observed fibrosis patterns, including "honeycombing" textures.
    *   Predicted fibrotic volume and location showed **very good agreement** with actual patient CT scans.
*   **Clinical Impact:** Potential to optimize radiotherapy planning to minimize fibrosis risk by personalizing dosage based on individual susceptibility models.

### 3. In-Silico Dynamics of Atrial Fibrillation and Fibrotic Remodeling
*   **Objective:** To evaluate how different types of fibrotic remodeling in low-voltage areas (LVA) affect atrial fibrillation (AF) dynamics and to interpret LVA findings from electroanatomic mapping.
*   **Methodology:**
    *   Created personalized electrophysiological models for **10 persistent AF patients**.
    *   Incorporated anatomical data, voltage mapping, and local activation times (LATs) at pacing intervals of 250ms and 600ms.
    *   Simulated three fibrosis types: slow conduction velocity, ionic channel changes, and replacement fibrosis (probabilistic percolation).
    *   Analyzed wavefront propagation and rotational activity after simulated pulmonary vein isolation.
*   **Results:**
    *   Rotational activity locations varied significantly based on fibrosis type and pacing rate (e.g., anterior wall vs. posterior wall).
    *   Models with **ionic changes** resulted in AF termination in over 50% of cases.
*   **Key Insight:** Low-voltage areas have complex impacts on AF maintenance; incorporating ionic remodeling may improve the understanding of arrhythmia susceptibility and ablation outcomes.

### 4. Finite Element Simulation of the Pseudophakic Eye
*   **Objective:** To understand the biomechanical factors contributing to late in-the-bag intraocular lens (IOL) dislocation, particularly in eyes with pseudoexfoliation.
*   **Methodology:**
    *   Performed **Finite Element (FE) analysis** of healthy, cataract, and pseudophakic states under intact and pseudoexfoliative conditions.
    *   Emulated zonular weakness by progressively reducing zonular thickness.
    *   Assessed stress distribution under gravity loads and varying IOL positions.
*   **Results:**
    *   Gravity had minimal impact on the pseudophakic complex.
    *   **Horizontal IOL position** resulted in the least stress.
    *   Pseudoexfoliation significantly heightened zonular stress in affected areas, potentially driving disease progression.
    *   Pseudophakic eyes exhibited increased capsular stress, offering insights into "dead bag syndrome."
*   **Clinical Impact:** Findings support surgical planning strategies, such as optimizing IOL positioning, to mitigate dislocation risks in degenerative conditions.

### 5. Ranolazine Effects on Arrhythmic Substrate in Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
*   **Objective:** To investigate the substrate mechanisms of ranolazine (late Na+ channel blocker) in HCM, specifically regarding its safety and efficacy across different degrees of repolarization impairment.
*   **Methodology:**
    *   Used computational models of HCM tissue and ventricles with variable repolarization impairment.
    *   Applied S1-S2 pacing protocols to quantify arrhythmic risk.
    *   Simulated treatment with **3μM, 6μM, and 10μM ranolazine**.
    *   Derived ECG biomarkers from biventricular simulations.
*   **Results:**
    *   Ranolazine prevented ventricular tachycardia (VT) in 53%, 73%, and 80% of models at increasing concentrations.
    *   **Optimal efficacy** was observed in moderate repolarization impairment (JT < 370ms).
    *   In severe-extreme impairment, ranolazine **increased arrhythmia sustainability** by decreasing refractory periods.
*   **Conclusion:** Ranolazine therapy must be tailored to the degree of repolarization heterogeneity; it may be beneficial in moderate cases but risky in severe-extreme cases.
